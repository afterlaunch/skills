# The axes

Ten dimensions the calibration test measures. Each has two poles and a
position between them. Nothing here is right or wrong; it is where a
particular voice sits.

**Only genuinely variable things belong here.** Any hard rules already
in force for this brand (no em-dashes, no aphorisms, no "it's not X
it's Y", a banned-vocabulary list, contractions on) are settled and
must not be re-litigated by a test item. Every option in every pair
should obey them, so the test measures taste inside the allowed space
rather than asking whether the rules apply.

| # | Axis | Pole A | Pole B |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Sentence length | Short, clipped, one clause | Longer, more clauses, flowing |
| 2 | Opening | Cold open straight on the claim | Context first, then the claim |
| 3 | Evidence order | Lead with the number | Lead with the situation |
| 4 | Certainty | Flat assertion | Measured, room for doubt |
| 5 | Address | Speaks to "you" | States to the room |
| 6 | Register | Spoken, casual | Written, precise |
| 7 | Technical density | Assumes the reader knows | Explains as it goes |
| 8 | Humour | Dry, occasional | Straight throughout |
| 9 | Self-reference | "I" and "we" visible | Subject forward, author invisible |
| 10 | Closing | Ends on the point | Ends on an invitation |

## Writing a test item

One pair, two sentences, **one axis varying and everything else held
still**. Same subject, same length bracket, same register except on the
axis being tested. If two things vary, the answer tells you nothing.

Subject matter should be plausibly something this brand might say,
whatever its own territory is: a product decision, an industry trend,
a customer's problem, shipping something. **The facts do not matter
and can be invented**, because nobody is being asked whether the claim
is true. They are being asked which sentence sounds more like them.

Bad item, two axes moving at once:

> A. "Most founders get this wrong."
> B. "In our experience, a considerable number of founders tend to
>     approach this in a way that turns out to be counterproductive."

That varies length AND certainty AND register. Useless.

Good item, certainty only:

> A. "Most founders get this wrong."
> B. "Most founders seem to get this wrong."

Same length, same register, same opening. One thing moved.
