# Tells

The one register of patterns that give machine-made work away. Prose tells
and visual tells in one file, because they are the same problem wearing
two different disguises and keeping two lists guarantees they drift
apart.

**The sourcing bar.** An entry earns its place one of two ways: it
appears on two or more independent public lists, or it is mechanically
checkable and has actually been caught on a real, shipped surface. Neither
"one person dislikes it" nor "it feels a bit AI" is enough. The bar exists
because a register that accepts taste becomes one person's aesthetics
wearing the authority of a rule, and then it starts overruling decisions
that were actually made on purpose.

**Sources.** The prose half was compiled from the banned-vocabulary lists
of ten public posting skills. The visual half was compiled from the
AI-tells section of `Leonxlnx/taste-skill` (MIT), sifted against the bar
above, plus patterns caught auditing real shipped pages.

**Who reads this file.** This skill (`tells`) audits any finished output,
prose or built surface, against the whole register. If you wire the
machine-checked subset (Part 3) into your own build's tests, read it from
this file directly rather than copying the patterns elsewhere, so the two
never drift apart.

That last point is the actual design: a rule nobody can forget beats a
rule everybody agrees with.

# Part 1: Prose

## Vocabulary, zero tolerance

leverage, unlock, delve, harness, robust, seamless, utilise, utilize,
empower, navigate (figurative), foster, streamline, fundamentally,
elevate, revolutionise, revolutionize, game-changer, cutting-edge,
best-in-class, industry-leading, transformative, holistic, synergy,
paradigm, ecosystem (figurative), supercharge, turbocharge, double-down,
deep dive, unpack (figurative), tapestry, testament, realm, landscape
(figurative), embark, journey (figurative)

## Phrases

"in today's landscape", "in an increasingly", "it's not just X, it's Y",
"let that sink in", "the truth is", "here's the thing", "at the end of the
day", "moving the needle", "circle back", "low-hanging fruit", "the
reality is", "make no mistake", "that said" as a paragraph opener

## Punctuation and typography

- **Em-dash.** Zero, always. Use a full stop and a new sentence, a colon,
  or a comma.
- Semicolons in social copy read as drafted rather than spoken.
- Curly quotes mixed with straight ones.
- Bold used for emphasis more than once or twice.

## Constructions, zero tolerance

The four shapes that give a post away fastest. Full reasoning in the
suite's `../draft/STYLE.md`; these are the enforcement lines.

- **Aphorisms.** Any sentence built to be quotable rather than to say
  something. If it would fit on a quote card, it fails.
- **This, but that.** "It's not X, it's Y", "Not a tool. A system.", and
  every variant of the reversal. The most overused construction in AI
  social copy.
- **The pithy fragment.** A clipped ending for emphasis. "Simple, right?",
  "Every time.", "That's it.", "Not close."
- **Stacked fragments.** "Fast. Simple. Done." Three beats where a
  sentence would do.

## The essay machinery

Five further patterns, added after a draft that passed every entry above
still read as machine-made. One instance of any of these is a judgement
call; recurrence fails the draft.

- **Enumeration scaffolds.** "Three measurements change what that means.
  The first is... The second is... The third..." Announcing a list, then
  walking it with ordinals. Say the things and let the reader count.
- **Meta-connectives.** "Two things are true at once.", "Two details
  stand out.", "Here is where it gets interesting." Sentences that
  announce structure instead of carrying content. Delete them; if the
  paragraph stops making sense without them, the structure was
  load-bearing and the fix is the paragraph, not the connective.
- **The X-not-Y pivot.** "Measured, not projected." "Definitional, not a
  contradiction." The reversal's smaller sibling: a clipped contrast doing
  rhetoric instead of work. Recast with "rather than", or give each half
  its own plain sentence.
- **Uniform tidy landings.** Every paragraph clicking shut on a polished
  final beat. Human paragraphs sometimes just stop; let a few. Sibling of
  "a closing line that restates the opening" below, one level down.
- **Uncontracted prose, widened.** The register entry below says casual
  writing specifically; the pattern is broader than that. Any prose meant
  to read as written by a person wants natural contractions mixed with
  formal forms. All full forms is a tell, all contractions is a different
  tell; uniform anything is the tell.

## Register

- **Uncontracted forms in casual writing.** "would not" where "wouldn't"
  belongs. The register does not match the room.
- Any adjective not carrying a fact.
- Throat-clearing before the point.
- A closing line that restates the opening.

## Structure

- Uniform sentence length across a whole post
- Rule-of-three lists everywhere, especially three adjectives
- Intro, balanced body, summary, optimistic close
- Every paragraph the same shape
- A closing line that restates the opening in different words
- Symmetrical thread posts, each the same length

## Tone

- Hedging on every claim
- Balance for its own sake, presenting two sides where one is right
- Enthusiasm with no specific object
- Advice with no failure mode attached
- Authority claimed rather than shown

# Part 2: Visual and layout

For built surfaces: marketing pages, app screens, anything rendered. Same
idea as the prose half. These are the moves a model reaches for when it is
trying to look designed, which is why they cluster.

## Labels that enumerate

- **Generic step labels.** `STEP 1`, `Stage 2`, `Phase 01` above a
  heading. If the steps are already in order on the page, the order
  already says it. The step's own title is the label. *(Machine-checkable.)*
- **Section-number eyebrows.** `001 / Capabilities`, `06 · how it works`.
  An eyebrow should name the topic in words or not exist. **A carve-out
  exists:** a numbered eyebrow that is actually wired to real in-page
  navigation, a chapter rail with matching numbers elsewhere on the page,
  is wayfinding, not decoration. If you have deliberately built that, it
  wins over this entry. Do not "fix" it.
- **Pagination on things you can count.** `01 / 4` under four tiles.
- **Numbered figure plates.** `Figure 01 · The timeline` above a chart or
  exhibit. The same tell as the section-number eyebrow, wearing academic
  clothes. Name the chart in words, and cross-reference it as "the chart
  below" rather than by number.

## Decoration pretending to be content

- **The mono strip.** A small letter-spaced triad across the foot of a
  hero or section: `DESIGN · BUILD · SHIP`. Agency-portfolio cliché. Say
  the thing in a sentence or cut the line.
- **Separator abuse.** The middle dot as the default joiner for
  everything. Ration it to one per line in metadata. Terminal and
  window-chrome mock content is exempt: a dot separator is authentic
  there.
- **Decorative status dots.** A coloured dot before every nav item or
  list row. Only for real semantic state, sparingly.
- **Scroll cues.** `Scroll to explore`, an animated mouse wheel. If the
  reader has not scrolled, they are looking at the hero. They know.
  *(Machine-checkable.)*
- **Locale and weather strips.** `LIS 14:23 · 18°C` in a header, unless
  the brief is genuinely about a place.
- **Version stamps on marketing.** `v1.4.2`, `Build 0048` in a footer.
  Devtool fixtures, not landing-page content.

## Fabricated substance

- **Placeholder identities.** Acme, Jane Doe, John Doe, lorem ipsum.
  Names should be plausible and specific. *(Machine-checkable.)*
- **Fake-perfect numbers.** `99.99%`, exactly `50%`. Real data is untidy.
  The safer rule is stricter still: never invent a figure at all, tidy or
  not.
- **Div-built product previews.** A fake dashboard or terminal made of
  styled rectangles, called out as the single biggest visual tell by the
  source this half was sifted against. An honestly labelled illustrative
  preview (a visible "sample data" or "illustrative" tag) is the
  legitimate form of the same pattern; losing that label is what turns it
  into a tell.

## Layout habits

- **Three equal feature cards.** The generic identical-trio row.
- **Eyebrow on every section.** Roughly one per three sections is the
  ceiling. If a section needs a category label, its position on the page
  usually already gives it one.
- **Split headers.** Big headline left, small floating explainer
  paragraph top-right. Stack it instead.
- **Layout repetition.** The same section shape more than twice in a row.
  Nine sections want at least four different layout families.
- **Empty bento cells.** The grid should have exactly as many cells as
  there is content for.

## What this register deliberately does not cover

Type choices, palette families, specific banned typefaces, motion
intensity. These are taste, not tells; if your own design direction has
already settled them, that ruling wins. A register that reaches into
settled design decisions gets ignored wholesale, and then it stops
catching the real tells too.

# Part 3: Machine-checked

The subset worth wiring into an actual build test. Keep this list
deliberately small: every entry here must be greppable, genuinely at zero
on a page you are happy with, and a real tell rather than a preference.
Adding one means proving all three.

The JSON below is written so a test can parse it directly rather than
duplicating the patterns by hand.

```json
{
  "scope": "<path to your own marketing components>",
  "extensions": [".tsx", ".ts", ".jsx", ".html"],
  "rules": [
    {
      "id": "generic-step-label",
      "pattern": "([\"'>])\\s*(?:STEP|Step|STAGE|Stage|PHASE|Phase)\\s*0?\\d\\s*([\"'<])",
      "why": "A step label enumerates what the page order already says. The step's own title is the label."
    },
    {
      "id": "scroll-cue",
      "pattern": "(?:\\u2193\\s*[Ss]croll|[Ss]croll to explore|[Ss]croll to walk|[Ss]croll down to)",
      "why": "If the reader has not scrolled yet they are looking at the hero. The bottom of the viewport does not need a label."
    },
    {
      "id": "placeholder-identity",
      "pattern": "\\b(?:Acme|John Doe|Jane Doe|[Ll]orem ipsum|SmartFlow|Cloudly)\\b",
      "why": "Placeholder names read as unfinished. Use plausible, specific ones."
    }
  ]
}
```

**Scope note.** Point `scope` at whatever directory holds your actual
marketing components, not your whole codebase; a devtool fixture or a
test-data file legitimately contains the strings above. If an older part
of your own site still carries a pattern this register now bans, that is a
known, tracked gap, not something this file will pretend is clean.

## The test that is not a rule

Read it aloud, or look at it cold. Machine work is evenly weighted and
nothing surprises you. If no sentence makes you stop, and no part of the
page makes you look twice, neither will do it for a reader.

**Do not manufacture roughness.** Adding a flaw to seem human is itself a
machine behaviour, and a regular one becomes a new tell.
