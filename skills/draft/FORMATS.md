# Formats and the mix

Different kinds of posts need very different kinds of material, and they
take very different amounts of work to produce, which is why a fixed
ratio matters. Without a target mix, what you publish drifts toward
whatever is easiest that day, and the easiest formats are usually the
ones anyone could write.

## Naming your own formats

Sort what you could publish into a small number of named formats (four or
five is plenty), each tagged by what it actually needs:

| Format | Needs | How much work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A finding | Something you actually measured or tested | Real, sometimes substantial, work |
| A shift | Nothing but your own years in the work | Free, and only you have it |
| A how-to | Craft, given away whole | Free |
| A comparison | Real data behind it, dated and specific | Real work |
| A receipt | A shipped thing with a genuine before and after | Free, but rare |

The share each format gets is yours to set from your own ledger; a
reasonable starting instinct is to weight it toward whatever only you can
produce, since that is the actual reason anyone follows you rather than a
search result.

**Rule:** if the last few entries in your ledger all needed nothing but
you or nothing at all, the next one should draw on something real. If the
format that needs real material is blocked because you have nothing to
report, say so rather than quietly writing another easy one.

## A finding

What you actually found when you measured or tested something. Needs a
completed piece of work behind it: a study, a scan, an experiment, a
genuinely run comparison. Report the one number that is actually
comparable, not a rate that looks comparable but was measured under
different conditions; state the comparison's scope in the same sentence
as the number.

Shape: the finding, then the field around it, then what it means for the
reader.

## A shift

Something used to be true. It is not any more. Here is what to do
instead.

Needs nothing but you. This is the format that carries your actual
thesis, and the only one where your own years in the work are the entire
evidence.

Shape: the old world in one line, the change in one line, the consequence
for the reader in two.

## A how-to

One concrete action, explained well enough that someone could do it
today.

Needs nothing. Give it away completely, including the last step. Do not
withhold the ending to make someone book a call with you.

Shape: the situation it fits, the steps, the failure mode most people
hit.

## A comparison

Two things that should not both be true, sitting next to each other.

Needs real, dated, specific data. This is the most striking format
available and also the most legally sensitive one: report what you
observed, dated, and let the reader draw the conclusion. "Named fourth in
its own category" is an observation. "Losing badly" is a verdict on
someone, and it is not yours to hand down.

Shape: the number, the field it sits in, why it matters, its caveat.

## A receipt

This shipped. Here is what moved.

Needs a genuine before and after. Rare, and worth waiting for rather than
manufacturing one.

Shape: what changed, what you did, over what period, and what you still
do not know.
