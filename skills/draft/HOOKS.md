# Hooks

Never improvise an opening. Every posting skill worth studying uses a
named formula library, and generating options then choosing beats writing
one finished post.

**Always produce three, with one line each on why it might work.** Then
pick, or let the founder pick.

On a professional network the fold sits wherever the platform currently
puts its "see more" cutoff, which moves and is worth checking on the day
rather than trusting from a list. On a short-form platform it is the first
line. The hook does all the work either way.

## The formulas

**The number.** Open on a figure, precise rather than round. A decimal
reads as measured; a round number reads as estimated. Only ever a number
you can actually stand behind.
> Template: "[Something] showed up in [N] of the [total] answers we
> checked." Fill in your own real count; never write a number here you
> have not actually produced.

**The mismatch.** State two things that should not both be true.
> Template: "[The thing everyone assumes] and [the number that says
> otherwise] disagree." Real numbers only.

**The retired belief.** Name a practice, say it is finished, and
immediately say what replaces it. Without the replacement it is a
complaint.

**The shift.** Past state, present state, consequence for the reader. The
safest opening in the set, and the only one that needs nothing but your
own years in the work.
> Shape: "[This] used to be true. [This] changed. Here is what to do about
> it."

**The quiet observation.** Something true and slightly uncomfortable that
the reader already half knows.
> "Everyone can [do the easy part] now. Almost nobody can [do the hard
> part]."

**The labour done for you.** You ran something so the reader does not have
to, and here is what came back. Compounds well, because it makes you the
standing source rather than a one-off.

## Banned openings

- "Confession:" and "I have never shared this publicly." Overused enough
  to be recognisable, and it sits close to the self-denigration line.
- "Let me tell you about", "Here's a thread", "1/7", "A thread" with a
  thread emoji.
- Any question whose only purpose is to bait a reply.
- Anything that promises value later instead of delivering some now.
